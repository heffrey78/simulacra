> **Status: done.** 894 tests green, up from 863. This milestone answers
> every finding of the [2026-09-11 review](../playtests/2026-09-11.md): the
> first play of M14, and the first on `qwen3.5:2b`. One of the fixes, arming

**Goal:** every line the review quoted reads correctly the next time it
happens, and floors 1–3 have a weapon without changing any existing world.

**Constraint:** no new model calls. Two of the fixes change what reaches a
prompt; the rest are code deciding what code should have decided already.

**F1 is the base game, not loot.** It is the design's one weapon per floor, a
bug since M1, which M14 found and recorded as O18. So it has its own stream,
and the balance simulation's loot-free baseline includes it. Re-roling the
colliding rooms would have been the obvious fix, and it would have changed
the rooms of every existing world, whose names and concepts were written for
the old roles.

**F8 has a limit.** Verbs turn and pronouns don't: *"I remember exactly what
each delver owes her"*. Getting pronouns right means knowing who each one
refers to. The slip reads as a slip, not as another person speaking.

## Findings

### Arming floors 1–3 moves the curve two floors

Every run in M3's simulation fought bare-handed on floors 1–3, because M1's
slot collision had always been there. So M3's "median of 4" was calibrated on
an unarmed early game. With the weapon the design always meant those floors to
have (400 runs each):

| | median floor | p90 | dead by floor 2 | worst floor's share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unarmed, no loot (M3's calibration) | 4 | 7 | 3% | 32–35% |
| armed, no loot | 6 | 8 | 1% | 23% |
| **armed, with loot (as shipped)** | **6 (Hardpan 7)** | **8** | **1–2%** | **29–32%** |

Every one of M3's curve tests still passes. Runs end: the maximum is 10, and
p90 is 8 against a bound of 25. Early deaths are 1–2% against 15%. No floor
takes a third of the deaths, and runs end on ten different floors. The curve
is two floors longer, and it still ends at the wall where the strong tier
arrives.

**That makes it a design decision.** If two floors is too long, the knobs are
M3's, not loot's: monster scaling per floor, the tier bands, or deliberately
leaving floor 1 unarmed.

### Loot, re-tuned against the armed game

On the armed base, M14's rates put Hardpan's median at +2 on the test's
200-run sample, over the bound. The median sits on a boundary there: the same
rates give +1 at 400 runs. This grid ran on the shipped code:

| drops | boss | caches | holds within one floor, both themes, at 200 and 400 runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 / 25 / 40% | 50% (M14) | 10% | **no**: Hardpan +2 at 200 |
| **15 / 25 / 40%** | **35%** | **10%** | **yes** |
| 10 / 20 / 30% | 50% | 10% | yes |
| 10 / 20 / 30% | 35% | 10% or 5% | yes |
| 15 / 25 / 40% | 25% | 5% | yes |

Only the boss moved, because the boss was the knob M14 turned up *to arm floors
1–3*, and F1 now does that job directly. Drops and caches are unchanged. A
boss also never drops less often than its own tier would: 35% is under the
strong tier's 40%, so without that rule a strong-tier boss would drop less
often than an ordinary strong monster.

### F8, measured live: no difference, and a bigger problem

I asked the Widow and the Assayer "Who are you?" five times each on
`qwen3.5:2b`: once with their canon as authored, once as F8 hands it over.

| | as authored | first person |
|---|---|---|
| replies opening in the first person | 9 of 10 | 10 of 10 |
| canon handed to the *delver* ("You keep a plate warm…") | 1 of 10 | 1 of 10 |

The Widow's slip went (1 → 0), and the Assayer produced one of its own in the
first-person set: *"You have been paid nothing since the mail ceased"*. At five
samples a cell this says nothing either way. F8 stays, because it is still
right to hand a model data it can say as written. But it did not measurably fix
what the playtest saw.

**What the 2b actually does wrong is invent, and take its role literally.**
Every one of the Assayer's ten replies named a price the canon never gave:
*"one hundred dollars per ton"*, *"eight dollars and forty cents"*, *"two cents
per pound"*. Four called it an object rather than a person: *"I am a scale"*,
*"a precision instrument"*, *"a sack of iron"*. The Widow named herself
*"Elara"*. The Assayer's role is *"weighs what you bring up and tells you what
it is worth"*, and a 2b reads that as a job to perform. That's the next thing
for dialogue: an instruction or a guard against inventing numbers, and a role
line that says who, not only what. It needs its own measured pass.

### Existing worlds

The 80 floors snapshotted before M14 (both themes, four world seeds, depths
1–10) regenerate with every stream. No room differs in layout, names,
monsters or original items. 24 weapons were added after the originals, one
for each vault-less floor.
