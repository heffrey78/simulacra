## F1 — The fallback may only pick `look` or `move` for a look or a move

**Files:** [engine/parser.py](../../src/simulacra/engine/parser.py)

`jump`, `slam`, `water`, `eat tin of peaches` all came back as the room
description; `dodge` and `/exit` came back as *moves*, and `dodge` walked the
player into the room that killed them. The fallback's verb list has grown from
10 to 18 across M7–M11 — the wide-choice problem M3 measured — and the guards
behind it only checked that a direction was real, not that the player typed one.

- `look` is accepted only if the player typed a looking word, or a target they
  named. Otherwise it is an improvisation.
- `move` is accepted only if the player typed the direction.
- M11's look-target guard dropped the invented target but kept the verb, which
  landed the request on the room description. It now degrades to improvise, the
  same as the `take`/`use` guard always has.

**Tests:** `jump` and `dodge` through a fallback that answers `look` and `move`
both reach the judge; `glance around` still looks.

## F3 — Three bugs the transcript showed

- **Transcripts collided across worlds.** Named by run number only, and run
  numbers restart with each new world, so three worlds' runs appended into one
  file. Name them by world as well.
- **"(remembers you)" after one conversation.** M8 set an NPC's recollections
  from any route's answer; room entry reads recollections for the tag, which is
  meant to mean a *previous run* was recalled.
- **The NPC prompt was ungrammatical for verb-phrase roles** — *"You are the
  Assayer. weighs what you bring up…"* — and the model echoed it back as
  *"You weigh what you bring up and tell you what it is worth."* Use
  *"You are {name}, who {role}."*, as the trade prompt already does.

### The one that got through: `/exit`

Found live, with the tests already green. The fallback answered `enter` with the
room's own name lifted from the summary. M11 added `enter`, which looks at its
target when that isn't a neighbouring room — and looking at the room's own name
is the room description. Fixed two ways: a leading `/` is a chat-client reflex
and is stripped, so `/exit` is just `exit`; and `enter` gets the same rule as
`look` — typed, or named, or it's an improvisation.

Worth recording because of *where* it came from. M11 added `enter` as a verb to
close exactly this class of bug, and in doing so opened a new door into it. Any
verb that falls back to looking is a way for a lifted target to reach the room
description, and each one needs the guard.

### The dev environment loses its extras

Mid-milestone, pytest disappeared from the venv. `uv run simulacra` syncs the
environment to the base dependencies, which uninstalls the `dev` extra (pytest)
and the `tui` extra (Textual) — so every plain `uv run` between test runs
silently removes both. `uv run --all-extras …` keeps them, and `.venv/bin/…`
doesn't sync at all. Moving the test dependencies to a uv dependency group,
which `uv run` keeps by default, would end it; not changed here, since it's a
project-configuration choice.

### Seen in the live check, carried forward

- **The judge's reason can promise what its vocabulary can't do.** `dodge` was
  ruled a hit — *"prevents enemy from attacking, reduces damage taken by 3"* —
  and the dry hand attacked on the same turn. Same family as M11's phantom
  enemy: the reason is free text, the effect is a closed enum, and nothing
  checks that they agree.
- **The judge can't see the pack.** `water` was ruled *"pouring water onto the
  floor to cool off"* with no water carried.
- **A recalled past run is narrated, not spoken.** `greet assayer` → *"The
  delver approached the Assayer on floor 1 and asked about assayer. They were
  killed by a falling weight."* The recollection was right; the voice was a
  narrator's, not the Assayer's.
- Still open from M11: a guard on the judge's reason, and M12's variety work —
  which this playtest has now shown is the engine's, not the simulacra theme's.
